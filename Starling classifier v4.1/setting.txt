The classification looks solid — brown ground, green vegetation, white/yellow bird contacts. The "fat" ground band (especially visible in images 1 and 4) is caused by the class threshold being too loose — it's catching low noise, grass tufts, and points sitting just above the true surface.

Settings to tighten the ground class
The key parameter is CSF Class threshold — it's the band (in metres) around the cloth surface within which points get labelled ground. In your build it also directly drives the final ground mask (|nZ| ≤ class_threshold).

Parameter	Current default	Change to	Effect
Class threshold	0.10 m	0.05 m	← main fix. Halves the ground band thickness
Cloth resolution	0.50 m	0.30 m	Cloth follows terrain tighter, won't bridge over low vegetation
Iterations	500	800	Cloth settles properly into the true surface
Rigidness	2	1	Keep at 1 — your scenes have real slopes; stiffer cloth would lift off and catch more
Pre-thin voxel	0.15 m	0.10 m	Keeps finer ground detail so the surface is accurate

Reality check
A terrestrial scan of natural ground (soil, grass, gravel) has genuine 3–8 cm roughness — you can't get a perfectly thin 1-point band, and you shouldn't try. If you push class_threshold below ~0.03 m you'll start losing real ground points.

If specific spots still over-catch after this:

Slopes catching vegetation (image 2) → drop cloth resolution to 0.25 m
Dense low scrub merging into ground (image 3) → that's genuinely ambiguous; CSF can't separate 5 cm scrub from ground reliably — would need a post-pass on intensity or return count
